# Ch.3 — Dry Dock (Day 4)

Minh did not go back under the bridge.

Booth 7 would still drip. The wall would still have his public death in the cache, a grey-hoodie corpse on NGH/USDC that the floor liked to replay. Harbor Node was still there — wifi, no questions, Oracle’s cracked tablet somewhere in the dark. He did not sit.

South bank. A quieter floor the regulars called Dry Dock: fewer espresso machines, worse gossip, a window that actually saw the channel. He kept the left cup on. The Tide Ledger sat inside the jacket, last page already spent. The backup wallet was the only live animal he had.

A North Pier runner found him before noon. Soft voice, fund badge, the kind of cover that smells like the people who wrote the fake Red Buoy. They had used his sixty-minute wait as camouflage. Now they offered a desk, a stack, a new name on their book.

He did not take it. He did not take Oracle Tide Signals. He did not take Harbor Node’s stool. The baby whale’s new side was narrower than a fund: against Lan, and not for North Pier either.

A cluster lit on his own screen — not Red Buoy, not hers. Thumbs wanted the entry. The rule had not died with the mentor: wait sixty minutes, or FOMO inverts the tide and burns what little backup liquidity he had left. He wrote the time. Entry: none.

Across the water, Harbor Node’s lights stayed on. Minh stayed flat at Dry Dock, dead on their wall, alive on a wallet the floor could not see.
