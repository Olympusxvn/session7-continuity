# Ch.1 — First clean Tide Signal (Harbor Node, Booth 7)

The drip from the bridge found Booth 7 the way gossip did: slow, then constant. Harbor Node’s wifi bars were full. Nobody asked Minh where the USDC in the grey-hoodie pocket had come from.

He kept the left earcup on, the right one off — Harbor Node etiquette, so you still heard the floor. Screen-light had already painted the usual red into his eyes. The Tide Ledger sat open beside the cracked saucer: paper, not a note app, because paper did not ping when your hands started lying.

Lan did not sit so much as occupy. Oracle, they called her upstairs; down here she was just Lan with a cracked exchange tablet and a habit of not blinking at candles. She angled the glass so he could see a cluster of wallets lighting up like buoys in a black channel.

“Red Buoy,” she said. “Textbook. NGH/USDC. Real whales parking, not a scripted pump — I’ve seen those. You don’t get to be clever tonight. You get to wait.”

The Tide Signal was a clean one. Minh felt the FOMO in his thumbs anyway: thin capital, one good entry, the whole baby-whale joke at this table turning into a real animal. He almost market-bought. The rule sat in his mouth like a coin: *do not open a position within sixty minutes of seeing it, or the tide inverts and burns your own liquidity.*

He wrote the time in the Ledger instead. Entry: none. Pair: NGH/USDC. Cluster: Red Buoy. Note: wait.

Lan watched the second hand like it owed her money. Around them the underground floor ticked — espresso, liquidations on other people’s screens, the bridge groaning when a truck hit the joint. Sixty minutes later the cluster had not faked. The tide held. Minh was still flat. Alive. Independent. A Harbor Node regular with a journal that had finally earned its first clean page.

He closed the Tide Ledger, backup-seed page still unopened at the back, and slid it into the backpack under the hoodie. Lan’s mouth almost smiled.

“That’s the read,” she said. “Tomorrow the water gets dirtier.”
